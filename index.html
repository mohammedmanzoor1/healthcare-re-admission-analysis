Explore the business questions

Eight questions the project set out to answer, rebuilt from the analytical result tables in the report.

What is the overall distribution of readmission outcomes?

54,861 encounters end with no readmission; the rest split between readmission after 30 days and within 30 days.

Which age groups have the highest 30-day readmission rates?

    The 20–30 age band has the highest displayed rate (14.24%), ahead of 80–90 (12.08%) and 70–80 (11.77%). That is worth noting against the report's key finding that older patients show higher readmission rates — the two largest older bands (60–90) are close behind, but 20–30 tops this table.

    Does a longer hospital stay increase readmission risk?

    Readmission rate (%) by time in hospital, 1–14 days

    The rate rises from 8.18% at 1 day toward the 12–14% range by 8–14 days, with some noise at the longer, thinner-data end of the scale.

      Small disposition groups swing to extremes (one group of 3 patients shows 66.67%), so the large groups — disposition 1 (60,232 patients, 9.3%) and disposition 3 (13,954 patients, 14.66%) — are the more reliable read.

      Number of diagnoses vs readmission rate

      Number of medications vs readmission rate

      Readmission rate climbs fairly steadily with the number of diagnoses, from 5.94% at one diagnosis to 17.65% at ten. Medication count shows a milder, noisier climb from 4.2% to just under 10%.

      Findings and features

      • Longer stays raise risk.Longer hospital stays are associated with an increased likelihood of readmission.
      • Utilization history matters.Patients with higher prior healthcare utilization show greater readmission risk.
      • Complexity matters.Readmission rates increase with the number of diagnosed conditions.
      • Admission and discharge category matter.Certain admission and discharge categories show significantly higher readmission rates than others.

        How it was built

        From raw encounters to an interactive dashboard.

        1. Clean the dataReplace "?" with nulls, drop columns with excessive missing values, remove identifiers and medication-specific fields, handle invalid records and duplicates, and standardize column names and types.
        2. Engineer featuresBuild the 30-day readmission flag, total prior visits, long-stay flag, polypharmacy flag and high-utilizer flag.
        3. Analyze in SQL ServerQuery encounter-level data to answer the business questions above.
        4. Build the Tableau dashboardA single-page dashboard with KPI cards and four core visualizations, filterable by race, gender and age.

        Conclusion

        This analysis identified several important drivers of hospital readmissions, including age, length of stay, prior healthcare utilization and clinical complexity. The insights can help healthcare organizations strengthen discharge planning, improve care coordination, and focus interventions on high-risk patient groups to reduce avoidable readmissions and improve patient outcomes.
